# R9700 capability profile and r4dx design principles

Scope: what the AMD Radeon AI PRO R9700 (gfx1201, Navi 48, RDNA4, 32 GiB, one card, HIP device 1,
Windows 11 + ROCm 7.15) can actually do, what Qwen3.8-27B actually costs on it, and the design
rules for r4dx that follow from the two. This document is the *decision layer* on top of three
research passes:

- `docs/research/r9700-arch.md` -- board specs and gfx12 ISA/builtin availability (cited below as
  **[ARCH §x]**, with the fact table at that doc's §6).
- `docs/research/r9700-microbench.md` -- on-device measurements taken 2026-09-19 (**[BENCH §x]**).
- `docs/research/r9700-kernel-lessons.md` -- libr4d kernel techniques + r4dx's own-kernel audit
  (**[LESSONS §x]**).
- `docs/perf.md` (**[PERF]**), `src/model/gemm_tuning_table.inc` (**[TUNE]**),
  `docs/container-format.md` (**[FMT]**), `docs/architecture.md` (**[ARCHDOC]**), and the engine
  source itself (`src/model/linear.cpp`, `src/model/gdn_layer.cpp`,
  `src/model/attention/include/r4dx/model/attention/linear.hpp`) for the engine's own numbers.

Every number is tagged **measured** (someone ran it on this card), **documented** (a published
spec), or **inferred** (arithmetic or reasoning by this document, from the two above). Inferred
numbers are the ones to attack with experiments -- §5 lists them.

Two rules this revision enforces throughout, because the previous revision broke both:

1. **Never mix step clocks.** [PERF]'s per-op profile is a *profiled* step (w4a16 wall 38.13 ms,
   `gpu_sum` 36.45 ms); the throughput table's steady-state step is 1/32.78 = 30.50 ms. Rescaling
   one onto the other and calling the result "measured" is not allowed. Where both appear, both are
   shown (§2.5), and the ~5.4 ms difference is itself an open question (Q2).
2. **[TUNE] is not in-model GEMM cost.** Its M=1 rows are repeat-loop benchmarks on single buffers,
   most of which are smaller than the 64 MiB MALL, and several back out *above* DRAM peak (§2.5,
   Q5). [TUNE] is a **ranking signal between kernel configurations**, never a cost model. Cost
   models in this document are byte rooflines.

### 1.2 Reading the profile

Four facts dominate every design decision below.

1. **The card's arithmetic:bandwidth ratio is 474 ops/byte** (C19 286 TOPS / C5 604 GB/s;
   **inferred**). A 4-bit weight carries 2 ops per byte-and-a-bit of storage, so any GEMM whose row
   count `M` is below ~40-90 is bandwidth-bound no matter how good the kernel is (§2.4).
2. **fp4 does not exist here** (C22). MXFP4 checkpoints must be software-dequantized to fp8 before
   the WMMA, which is exactly what `r4d_gemm_mxfp4a8_nt_m64` already does. There is no future
   RDNA4 kernel that makes MXFP4 cheaper than w4a8 -- both end at an 8-bit WMMA at the same
   documented dense peak (C37, 383 for both) and near-identical measured rates (C17 284 vs C19 286).
3. **The measured WMMA rates are 74-85% of the documented dense peaks** (C15-C21 vs C37). libr4d's
   quoted "issue rates" (207 / 412 / 407 / 830 TF/s) are each **1.06-1.08x above** the hardware's
   dense peak, so they are per-wave instruction-issue extrapolations, not sustainable device
   throughput. **This document never budgets against them** (Q6 asks only how much of the 25% gap
   between 286 TOPS and the 383 TOPS peak is contention vs real).
4. **Launches are cheap individually and expensive in aggregate.** 1.204 us completion floor (C30)
   x ~1200 launches/token is 1.4 ms of pure floor, but the *measured* excess over the byte roofline
   is ~12 us per non-GEMM launch in the profiled step (§2.5) -- an order of magnitude above the
   floor, because most of r4dx's own kernels are single-workgroup scalar loops.

### 2.2 Compute per token, the 16-row padding tax, and the draft-side budget

Useful FLOPs = 2 x 25.625 G params = **51.25 GFLOP per token** (inferred).

WMMA's minimum tile is 16 rows (C15-C21 are all `16x16x*`), and at decode M=1 the GEMMs issue a
full 16-row tile per output tile [LESSONS §1.1, §1.10]. So the *issued* work is 16 x 51.25 =
**820 GFLOP/token**, of which 15/16 is multiplying by zero-padding.

At C19 (286 TOPS int8) that padded work is **2.87 ms** -- 10.5% of the 27.33 ms bandwidth floor
below. **Decode is unambiguously bandwidth-bound, and rows 2..16 of every WMMA tile are free on the
verify side.** That is half of the economic case for MTP and DFlash2. The other half -- absent from
the previous revision -- is that **the draft side is not free**:

**Draft-side byte budget** (inferred, from [FMT]'s `mtp.*` = "same tensor set as one text layer"
and §2.1's per-block bytes):

| Draft component, per draft token | Bytes | ms @604 GB/s |
|---|---|---|
| MTP layer (one GDN-style layer 108.5 MB + one MLP 142.0 MB) | 250.5 MB | 0.41 |
| **`lm_head` full vocab** (248320x5120, the single most expensive GEMM in the model) | 675.4 MB | **1.12** |
| **Total per sequentially-drafted token** | **926 MB** | **1.53** |

Drafting is autoregressive: draft row `i+1` needs row `i`'s sampled token, so a width-`w` draft from
a single-head MTP is **`w` sequential M=1 passes**, not one M=`w` pass. Therefore:

- **MTP at w=2..5 (R8)**: 3.1-7.7 ms of draft per verify step, i.e. **10-25% overhead** on a 27-30
  ms step. Viable; priced into R8's gain column.
- **DFlash2 at w=10..16 (R9) with a sequential single-head drafter: not viable.** 15-24 ms of draft
  per step doubles the step to buy at most ~2 accepted tokens -- at p=0.5 acceptance that is *worse*
  than MTP at w=4. R9 requires either (a) a **multi-head/tree drafter that emits all `w` rows in one
  pass** (then the rows really are free, §2.4), or (b) a **reduced-vocab draft head** -- a top-8k
  `lm_head` is 41.9 M params = 22.3 MB = 0.04 ms, cutting the per-draft-token cost from 1.53 ms to
  **0.45 ms** and making w=16 cost 7.2 ms. **Pick one before writing any DFlash2 code** (Q10).

**State-rollback budget** (inferred): the KV cache rolls back for free (discard slots), but GDN's
recurrent state is *updated in place*. Rejecting draft rows needs either a checkpoint of the 151 MB
fp32 state before each verify step (**302 MB r+w = 0.50 ms/step = ~1.7%** -- cheap, and the
recommended default, P10) or a verify kernel that only commits the accepted prefix. The conv state's
rolling-buffer depth must also grow with the verify width (`conv_width - 2 + max_decode_window`,
[PERF]'s review-fix item 1 -- an off-by-one here was already a blocker-class bug once).

### 2.4 Crossover row count M* per GEMM kernel

Analytic: a 4-bit GEMM flips from bandwidth- to compute-bound when
`2 * M_eff * N * K / R = B * N * K / BW`, i.e. **`M* = B * R / (2 * BW)`** where `B` is bytes/param,
`R` is the *achieved* matrix rate and `M_eff = ceil(M/16)*16`. Achieved `R` at M=64 is backed out of
[TUNE]'s `mlp.gate_up` row (2 x 64 x 34816 x 5120 = 22.82 GFLOP per call) -- a *relative* use of
[TUNE], which is legitimate:

| Layout | WMMA type | Achieved R @M=64 (`gate_up`) | Analytic M* | Empirical M* (first M in [TUNE] more than 10% above the M=1 plateau) | Cost @M=64 vs M=1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| w4a16 | f16 (C15) | **64.0 TF/s** (356.43 us) = 34% of the 191 TF/s peak | 28 | `gate_up` ~18-24, `down` ~20, `in_proj_qkv` ~18 | **2.41x** |
| w4a8 | iu8 (C19) | **93.0 TF/s** (245.34 us) = 24% of the 383 TOPS peak | 40 | `gate_up` ~32, `down` ~40, `in_proj_qkv` ~40 | **1.68x** |
| mxfp4 | fp8 (C17) | 85.8 TF/s (265.91 us) | 38 | `gate_up` ~24, **`down` ~12**, **`in_proj_qkv` ~8-10** | 1.62x |
| bf16 | f16 | 33.0 TF/s (691.99 us, still bandwidth-limited) | n/a -- 2 B/param pushes it past 64 | none below 64 | 1.23x |

(The analytic and empirical columns disagree because the empirical one includes the 16-row rounding
and because achieved `R` is lower near the knee than at M=64. **Use the empirical column for
routing.**)

**The operational conclusion, correctly scoped**: `M* >= 16` **for w4a8 and w4a16**, so every verify
width r4dx will use (MTP 2-5, DFlash2 10-16) costs the same bytes as a single decode step on those
two layouts. From [TUNE], the whole-model quantized-GEMM sum for w4a8 is 19.10 ms at M=1 and
19.70 ms at M=16 -- **+3.1% for 16x the rows**. **mxfp4 is different**: its `down` and
`in_proj_qkv` knees sit at ~8-12 rows (`mlp.down` 50.53 us at M=1 -> 59.98 us at M=16, +18.7%), so
mxfp4 pays a real per-row cost across the whole DFlash2 band. That is an independent argument for
P1's choice of w4a8 as the verify-path layout. The bf16 `in_proj_z`/`k`/`v` GEMMs are bandwidth-bound
to M=64 and are free at every verify width either way.

### 2.6 Prefill ceiling

- **Bandwidth time/token** = 26.68 ms / T (w4a16), 26.06 ms / T (w4a8).
- **Compute time/token** = 51.25 GFLOP / R_eff. At R_eff = 186 TOPS (65% of C19's **measured** 286;

| Regime | Ceiling |

## 3. Design principles for r4dx

Each principle names the fact that forces it.

### P1. Default weight layout is **w4a8**; route by M, never by prefill/decode

**Fact**: w4a8 stores the byte-identical `wq` as w4a16 with a 2-byte-per-128 scale instead of 4
([FMT]), so it moves **2.3% fewer bytes per token than w4a16/mxfp4** (15.743 vs 16.116 GB) -- worth
0.62 ms/token = ~+0.7 tok/s for free. Above M*, int8 WMMA (C19 286 TOPS measured, 383 documented
peak) is the fastest path the hardware offers for a 4-bit checkpoint, and [TUNE] shows w4a8 at
**1.45x** w4a16 on `mlp.gate_up` @M=64 (245.34 vs 356.43 us) and **1.81x** on `mlp.down` (119.41 vs
216.56 us), while already being marginally *faster* at M=1 on every shape. And mxfp4's M* of ~8-12
on two shapes (§2.4) makes it the wrong layout for any verify width above ~8.

- **w4a8 is the default container layout.** w4a16 becomes the accuracy-comparison layout, mxfp4 the
  checkpoint-compatibility layout, bf16 the numerical reference (which does not fit, §2.1).
- Routing inside a layout is by `M` band only, through the existing `PickTuning` table. The bands
  that matter: `M in [1]` decode, `M in [2,5]` MTP verify, `M in [6,16]` DFlash2 verify, `M in
  [17,64]` prefill chunk. All four are below or near M* for w4a8 (§2.4), so **all four are the same
  bandwidth-bound kernel** -- the table only picks `WV/SK/NPW`.
- Do **not** add a per-M layout switch (e.g. "w4a16 at decode, w4a8 at verify"): that would need two
  copies of ~15 GiB of weights.
- **The bf16-only linears are part of the layout decision too.** `in_proj_z`/`k`/`v` are 20.6% of the
  token in every layout (§2.1) and are *not* covered by `--layout`. Quantizing them (R1) is worth
  more than every kernel item in §4 combined.

### P2. Fuse activation quantization into its producer (norm, silu, gated-norm)

**Fact**: **257** quant/cast launches per decode token (`linear.cpp:107-128`, one per quantized GEMM
-- §2.5's census), each costing up to 27/35/55 us (w4a16/w4a8/mxfp4) against kernels that move
10-35 KB; r4dx's `QuantActFp8Kernel` launches `grid = dim3(M)` = **one workgroup** at decode
[LESSONS §2].

- Every producer of a GEMM input already computes a per-row reduction (`RmsNormKernel`,
  `ResidualRmsNormKernel`, the GDN gated norm) or already touches every element (`SiluMulKernel`).
  The absmax pass is free inside them.
- **There is no duplicate-quantization win** (§2.5): each normed row feeds exactly one quantized
  GEMM, because `in_proj_z/a/b` and `k/v` are bf16. Fusion is the only lever here.
- **int8 path**: the fused epilogue must emit bytes **already in `fragA8` WMMA-fragment order**, not
  row-major -- [LESSONS §1.11] measured the strided alternative at 106.7 us vs 96.5 us at M=64.
- **fp8 path**: plain row-major e4m3 with `scale = max(1e-8, absmax)/448`, **no** fragment reorder --
  `r4d_gemm_mxfp4a8_nt_m64` reads A row-major (`A + r*K + kg + mhalf`) [LESSONS §1.11].
- While in there, fix the scalar loads: use `ushort4`/`uint4` 16-byte loads and `f2bf2`/`f2bf4`
  packed converts (2 adds + one `v_perm_b32`, C24) the way every libr4d kernel does
  [LESSONS §1.2, §2].
- **`r4dx_residual_rmsnorm_bf16` already exists and is never called** [LESSONS §3, §5.1]. Wiring it
  into the two layer boundaries removes 128 launches/token for zero new kernel code.

### P3. Capture the decode step and the verify step as HIP graphs -- after making the step device-resident

**Fact**: graph replay is 0.712 us/launch vs 1.204 us (C30/C31), **and hipGraph demonstrably works
on this Windows stack** (C32 -- this resolves [ARCH §4]'s open question). At ~1200 launches that is
a **0.59 ms/token** GPU-side saving. It is *not* also a host-enqueue saving: `host_enqueue` is
2.48-4.25 ms against a `finish_wait` of 93.5% of wall (C35), i.e. **the host issues and then
blocks -- it is not the critical path today** ([BENCH §3b] independently estimates graphs at ~1% of
a decode step). Revisit that when the step reaches ~20 ms.

- Decode is otherwise the ideal graph: fixed shapes, fixed arena addresses (`Arena::Reset()` is a
  host-side offset rewind, so device pointers repeat exactly), fixed control arrays
  (`GdnControlCache` already uploads each `(T,slot)` once, ever [PERF]).
- **Three things break the capture, and all three must be fixed first:**
  1. **The per-token embedding upload** (C39): a 10 KiB H2D from *pageable* host memory every step.
     Either move `text.embed_tokens` into VRAM and gather on-device (2.543 GB bf16; 15.01 weights +
     2.37 embed + 4.0 KV@131k + ~0.7 arena = **22.1 of 31.86 GiB, still fits**) or pin a staging
     buffer. The VRAM option is strictly better: it makes the whole decode graph device-resident,
     which is what this principle wants anyway.
     **Done and re-measured (2026-09-20, FIX pass): the mirror shipped (stage 1, `ced8acc`,
     `ModelOptions::embed_device_resident`, default on) and folds into the SAME `weights=` bucket
     `Model::Load`'s breakdown reports, not a separate line -- measured `weights=15.5076 GiB`
     (embed included) + `kv+gdn_state`/`arena+scratch` at `--max-ctx 2048` sums close to the
     `--stats`-reported 16.17 GiB used. This qualitative "still fits" conclusion holds; see the
     "VRAM reconciliation" blockquote above and docs/status.md/docs/perf.md for the corrected
     absolute figures across all three quantized layouts.**
  2. **The argmax D2H** (C33, 32.9 us): 0.11% of a 30.5 ms step today, but a captured graph cannot
     contain a host decision. Sampling must become device-side -- and **non-greedy sampling is not
     designed at all yet**; under speculation it becomes a per-verify-row accept/reject decision,
     which must also be device-side or the graph is pointless.
  3. The token id / position / sequence length, which must be device-resident (they nearly are) or
     updated via `hipGraphExecKernelNodeSetParams`.
- Capture **after** P2, not before: fusing first removes ~250 nodes from the graph.
- Capture the verify step separately, one graph per supported verify width.

### P4. fp8 KV is a bandwidth decision, not a capacity decision -- and it must stay fp8 at long context

**Fact**: KV is 32 KiB per token of context (§2.1). At 131072 tokens that is 4.295 GB read per decode
step = **20.7% of all bytes moved**, dropping the ceiling from 36.6 to 29.1 tok/s (§2.3). bf16 KV
would make it 8.59 GB = 34% and 23.6 tok/s.

- Capacity is not the constraint: 15.01 GiB weights + 4.0 GiB fp8 KV @131k + ~0.7 GiB arena = **19.7
  of 31.86 GiB** (the corrected weight figure happens to land in the same place as the old one).
  **The full 131072-token context fits with ~12 GiB to spare** -- enough to also hold the embedding
  table in VRAM (P3) and still have room.
  **Re-measured with P3 actually shipped (2026-09-20, FIX pass): weights=15.5076 GiB already
  includes the embedding mirror (measured, not the 15.01+2.37 additive estimate above -- see the
  P3 correction note), so the 131k-context sum is weights(15.5076, embed included) + fp8 KV@131k
  (4.295, §2.1's own more precise figure) + arena (~0.7-1.0) ~= 20.5-20.8 of 31.86 GiB -- still
  comfortably fits, ~11 GiB to spare, not materially different from this estimate's own
  conclusion.
  **R13/Q17 answered (2026-09-20, measured, real hardware -- this is no longer capacity
  arithmetic).** Decode at 131072 measures **31.09 tok/s** (94.0% of the R1-corrected 33.06 tok/s
  ceiling), not the 29.1 tok/s this section originally cited (that number used the pre-R1 base
  bytes/token, since superseded). Extended to the model's own native 262144-token ceiling (new):
  decode is **25.95 tok/s**, and the card still has **7.75 GiB (24%) free at `--mtp 0`, 6.83 GiB
  (21%) free at `--mtp 3`** -- see the VRAM-reconciliation blockquote in §2.1 and §2.3's own R13/Q17
  entry for the full measured table, the exact-match confirmation of the 32 KiB/token
  first-principles figure against the measured `hipMemGetInfo` delta, and the correctness evidence
  (needle retrieval + coherent haiku generation, both verified at every context length up to
  262144). `--max-ctx` now defaults to 262144 (was 131072) in both `src/cli/cli_args.h` and
  `src/server/server_args.h` as a direct consequence.
- Therefore the reason to keep fp8 KV is the ~21% of decode throughput it buys at long context, and
  any future "bigger KV" idea (bf16 KV for accuracy, larger head_dim) must be priced against that,
  not against VRAM.
- A 4-bit KV would buy a further 10% at 131k. Not worth the accuracy risk until 131k is a real
  workload -- **now that R13/Q17 confirm 131k (and 262k) fit and run correctly, this becomes a live
  question again, not attempted this pass.**

### P5. Non-temporal weight loads are a per-(shape, M) tuning entry, not a standing principle

**Fact, corrected**: nothing in a decode step is re-read *within* the step. Each weight byte is read
once, each KV byte is read once by its one attention layer, and the 3.146 MB/layer GDN state is read
and written by exactly one kernel (`r4d_gdn_recurrent_update`, which `gdn_layer.cpp:124-129` shows
also absorbs the gated norm). Reuse of KV and GDN state is *across steps*, and between two touches
16.5 GB streams through a 64 MiB MALL -- **256x the cache** -- so no temporal hint can preserve that
residency. The previous revision's justification ("NT protects the cache for KV and GDN state") and
its corollary ("order GDN kernels so the state stays cache-resident") were protecting reuse that
does not exist.

- The fact that actually supports NT is libr4d's **measured per-shape behaviour**: +3-4% at M=8/16,
  and a measured **loss** from M=24 up on long-K shapes (`r4d_gemm_w4a16_nt_m64.hip:77-84`). That
  makes `NT` a per-(shape, M) table entry -- i.e. exactly R12 -- not a global setting. Every [TUNE]
  row currently carries `NT=1` because the sweep never varied it.
- The real argument for ordering the GDN chain is **R6's fused kernel keeping state in registers/LDS
  across the conv/recurrent/gated-norm steps**, which is a different claim from cache residency and
  should be argued on its own terms.
- **MALL write behaviour is unexamined and is a genuine R6 design input**: the step writes 151 MB of
  GDN state plus all activations *through* a memory-side cache. Whether those stores allocate in
  MALL, and whether gfx12 exposes a store temporal hint worth using, is not covered by any of the
  three research docs (Q15).
- Corollary for benchmarking, **now fixed (Q5, Milestone 4, 2026-09-20)**: [TUNE]'s absolute
  microseconds for shapes under ~64 MiB used to be **Infinity-Cache-flattered** -- the pre-fix table
  had mxfp4 `mlp.down` at 50.53 us (implying 937 GB/s) and `gdn.in_proj_qkv` at 30.44 us (implying
  915 GB/s), both above DRAM peak (C4) and above measured stream (C5), exactly as
  `r4d_gemm_mxfp4a8_nt_m64.hip:81-84` warns. The full 280-row re-sweep with the ring-rotation fix now
  reads 84.43 us / 52.53 us for those same two cells -- both comfortably under the roofline. [TUNE]
  is still a **ranking signal, not a cost model** (its M=64 numbers are still repeat-loop benchmarks
  on however many buffers the ring needed, not an in-model measurement), but the specific
  above-DRAM-peak artifact that motivated this caveat is gone from the current table. See Q5's entry
  below for the full re-sweep and end-to-end re-measurement.

### P6. LDS budget, CU mode, and occupancy for r4dx-owned kernels

**Facts**: 64 KiB per block either mode (C11); WGP is the compile default (C28); LDS delivers
7.83 TB/s aggregate (C12); a 16x16x16 iu8 WMMA consumes 512 B of A+B operands per 8192 ops, so
**peak iu8 (C19) demands 17.9 TB/s of operand bytes** (inferred) -- **2.3x more than LDS can
supply**; `kkt_solve` at 190 VGPR runs 8 waves/SIMD naturally and **forcing 10 waves is 1.57x
slower** (39.9 vs 25.4 us) [LESSONS §1.3].

- **Register reuse must be >= 2.3x for any WMMA kernel we write** (that is what the 17.9-vs-7.83
  TB/s arithmetic actually requires); **design for 4x** as the margin, since C12 is a contended
  number that could move either way (Q12). 4x reuse means a **4x4 fragment register tile per wave**
  (64x64 output), costing 4x4x8 = 128 accumulator VGPRs + ~60 for operands/addressing ~= 190 VGPR
  -> **8 waves/SIMD** (C14). A 2x2 wave tile (32 accumulator VGPRs) gives only ~2x reuse -- below
  the requirement.
- **LDS budget target <= 32 KiB per block** so the default WGP mode (C28) keeps 2 blocks resident
  per WGP. Use `-mcumode` **per translation unit only** (the libr4d pattern, [ARCH §5.4]) and only
  for a kernel whose LDS footprint exceeds 32 KiB and that wants the CU's LDS bandwidth to itself --
  `r4d_gdn_chunk_scan` at ~61 KB is the one existing example.
- **Never set a `__launch_bounds__` minimum-waves above the compiler's natural VGPR-driven
  occupancy.** Measure, do not assume.
- For bandwidth-bound kernels (every decode GEMM, every fused elementwise kernel), the occupancy
  target is "enough loads in flight to cover DRAM latency": ~483 KB in flight at 604 GB/s x 800 ns
  (**inferred**, latency assumed), i.e. **>= 4 waves/SIMD with 16 B/lane loads, or fewer waves each
  with 4+ outstanding vector loads.** The current single-workgroup elementwise kernels (§2.5) miss
  this by ~64x.
- Replace `__syncthreads()` with a workgroup-scoped `lds_barrier()` wherever the barrier only orders
  LDS: on gfx12 `__syncthreads()` emits `global_inv scope:SCOPE_SE`, throwing away L0/L1 lines the
  kernel is about to reuse [LESSONS §1.6].

### P7. Idioms to use from libr4d

- **WMMA fragment layout `idx = lane % 16, k = 8*(e>>2) + 4*(lane>>4) + (e&3)`** is load-bearing
  everywhere; swapping WMMA operands transposes the result for free [LESSONS §1.1]. Keep all weight
  permutation **offline** (the container already does it) -- measured 1.15x at M=8 to 1.44x at M=64
  over reading checkpoint order.
- **`global_load_tr_b128`** (C25) only helps 16-bit operands needing a *runtime* transpose -- i.e.
  the attention V-fetch, which already uses it. There is no 4-bit or 8-bit transpose load and no LDS
  transpose at all (C26), so anything else must be permuted offline.
- **bf16 packing**: `f2bf2` = 2 adds + `v_perm_b32` (C24). The truncating `f2bf2t` variant is
  1.5-2.2% faster but moves the fp64-oracle error from 3.30e-3 to 9.02e-3/1.17e-2 -- **use it only
  for intermediates that do not feed a recurrence** [LESSONS §1.2]. Prefer f16 where numerically
  acceptable: `v_cvt_pkrtz_f16_f32` does two converts in one instruction.
- **`sched_group_barrier` coarsely**: group `(global load, LDS read, WMMA)` over 2-4 k-steps, not
  per step -- "the group barrier PINS the order at every boundary" [LESSONS §1.6]. Without it, LLVM
  single-buffers LDS and drains before every WMMA.
- **Split-K is not determinism-free**: a different `SK` changes the LDS reduction order and has
  already flipped generated tokens on the real model [PERF]. Pin `SK` per shipped tuning table and
  treat a retune as a behaviour change.

### P8. What NOT to build

| Do not build | Because |
|---|---|
| Any fp4 / fp6 / MX-scaled WMMA path | It does not exist on gfx1201 (C22). MXFP4 must dequantize to fp8 first, which `r4d_gemm_mxfp4a8_nt_m64` already does optimally (folded-exponent table, e4m3 subnormals honoured, [LESSONS §1.7]). |
| mxfp4 as the default layout | Same weight bytes as w4a16 (2.3% more than w4a8), same 8-bit WMMA rate (C17 vs C19), an M* of ~8-12 on two shapes (§2.4), and currently 27% slower end to end. Keep for compatibility only. |
| A **sequential** wide drafter for DFlash2 | §2.2: w sequential M=1 draft passes cost w x 1.53 ms, which at w=16 exceeds the whole verify step. Wide speculation needs a one-pass multi-head/tree drafter or a reduced-vocab draft head. |
| SWMMAC / structured sparsity (C23) | Requires 2:1-structured-sparse weights. This checkpoint is dense; making it sparse is a model-quality project, not a kernel project. |
| `iu4 x iu4` WMMA (C21, 592 TOPS) at decode | Decode is bandwidth-bound at M=1 (§2.2) -- 2x the matrix rate buys nothing. Only revisit **after** a prefill kernel exists and is measured compute-bound, and only with int4-activation accuracy evidence (Q11). Note C20: at K=16, iu4 is only 4% faster than iu8, so the win requires the K=32 form specifically. |
| P2P / multi-GPU | GPU RULE: device 1 only. Windows HIP lacks working IPC/P2P for this pattern, and at 4-bit the whole model plus a 131k KV cache fits in 19.7 of 31.86 GiB (P4). |
| Fine-grained / uncached device memory | Those allocations are GPU-uncached [ARCH §3]; on a 16.5 GB/token streaming workload that is catastrophic. |
| bf16-layout performance work | It over-commits VRAM by 1.50x and is being paged over PCIe (§2.1). Fixing its throughput means shrinking it, which is what the 4-bit layouts already are. |
| Cooperative-groups grid sync | Unknown Windows support [ARCH §4] and unnecessary: every kernel here is either embarrassingly parallel or per-layer serial. |
| Any cost model built on [TUNE] absolute microseconds | Rule 2 at the top of this document; §2.5; Q5. |

### P9. Prefill kernel specification

Only build this **after** Q7 (prefill per-op profile) shows the GEMM share is worth it (§2.6 says it
is ~36% today). When built:

- **Premise inversion.** Every skinny kernel assumes "the weight is read once and never reused, so
  never stage it in LDS" [LESSONS §1.10]. At T >= 128 a dequantized 16x16 weight tile feeds `T/16`
  row-tiles. The prefill kernel is a classically tiled GEMM, not a wider skinny kernel.
- **Block tile 128 (M) x 256 (N), K-step 64**, 8 wave32 waves per block arranged 2x4, each wave
  owning a **4x4 fragment register tile** (= 64x64 output, P6) -> 2x4 waves x 64x64 = 128x256 =
  exactly the block tile, at 8 waves/SIMD. (The previous revision specified a 128x128 block with the
  same 2x4 waves of 4x4 fragments, which tiles 32768 outputs into a 16384-output block -- arithmetically
  impossible. The alternative resolution, 128x128 with **four** waves of 4x4, is also legal and
  halves the LDS; it loses the 8-wave occupancy sweet spot, so it is the fallback, not the default.)
- **WMMA type per layout**: `iu8` for w4a8 (C19), `fp8_fp8` for mxfp4 (C17), `f16` for w4a16 (C15).
  The checkpoint format decides; fp8 and iu8 are the same speed. Reuse the existing dequant paths
  verbatim -- `dequant8()` (two ANDs + a shift) and `r4d_mxfp4_unpack8()` (folded-exponent table),
  both measured under a few percent of kernel time even at M=64 [LESSONS §1.7, §1.8].
- **LDS, recomputed for 128x256**: A-tile 128x64 int8 = 8 KiB, B-tile 256x64 int8 = 16 KiB,
  **double-buffered = 48 KiB** -- which exceeds the <= 32 KiB / 2-blocks-per-WGP target (P6), so
  either (a) stage B **raw 4-bit** and dequantize in registers: 8 + 8 = 16 KiB, **32 KiB
  double-buffered**, meeting the target exactly -- **this is the default** -- or (b) accept 48 KiB
  and one block per WGP, which needs measuring before it is chosen.
- **Double-buffering constraints specific to gfx12**: there is no direct-to-LDS / async copy and no
  `ds_read_tr` (C26), so the prefetch is an explicit `global_load -> VGPR -> ds_write` for buffer B
  while WMMAs consume buffer A. LLVM will single-buffer this unless told otherwise -- coarse
  `sched_group_barrier` groups over 2-4 k-steps (P7), a hard `sched_barrier()` between the load
  group and the WMMA group (without it, prefetch is inert [LESSONS §1.6]), and `lds_barrier()`
  rather than `__syncthreads()` (P6).
- **Drop split-K.** `SK` exists because "the independent N fragments do not fill the part" at small
  M. At T >= 128, `(N/256) x (T/128)` tiles already saturate 64 CUs, and dropping `SK` also removes
  the rounding-order-changes-output hazard (P7).
- **Expected throughput**: LDS supplies 7.83 TB/s (C12) against 17.9 TB/s of peak iu8 operand
  demand, so 4x register reuse makes the ceiling operand-feasible. Target **170-215 TOPS**, which is
  **44-56% of the 383 TOPS documented dense peak (C37)** and 60-75% of the 286 TOPS measured (C19).
  At 186 TOPS that is 51.25 GFLOP/token -> **275 us/token**, versus ~590 us/token of GEMM time
  today (§2.6): **~2.1x on the GEMM share**, which is ~36% of prefill.
- **Occupancy**: start at natural occupancy, no `__launch_bounds__` minimum (P6). Measure before
  forcing anything.

### P10. Speculation must budget the draft side and the recurrent state, not just the verify rows

**Fact**: §2.2. Rows 2..16 of a WMMA tile are free; *producing* those rows is not, and GDN's state
does not roll back for free.

- **Every speculation design must state three numbers before it is approved**: (1) draft bytes per
  step (`w` x 926 MB for a sequential full-vocab MTP drafter, or `w` x 273 MB with a top-8k draft
  head), (2) the state-rollback cost (0.50 ms/step for the 151 MB fp32 checkpoint, or "verify writes
  only the accepted prefix"), (3) the required acceptance rate for the whole thing to beat
  `1 / 30.5 ms`.
- **Default rollback strategy: checkpoint.** 302 MB r+w = 0.50 ms/step (~1.7%) is cheap, obviously
  correct, and independent of the verify kernel's internals. Only replace it with accepted-prefix
  commit if a measurement says the 1.7% matters.
- **The conv-state rolling buffer must be sized `conv_width - 2 + max_decode_window` with
  `max_decode_window` = the maximum verify width** ([PERF] review-fix item 1). This was already a
  blocker-class off-by-one once; widening speculation re-opens it.
- The accept/reject decision itself must be **device-side** (P3), or every verify step pays a
  32.9 us host round trip per row (C33).

## Revision notes

Changes in this revision, and why (each replaces something the previous revision asserted):

1. **Byte accounting rebuilt against [FMT].** `gdn.in_proj_z`, `gdn.in_proj_a/b`, `gdn.conv1d`,
   `attn.k`, `attn.v` and all norms are **bf16-only, in every layout**. Weights are **16.116 GB
   (15.01 GiB)**, not 13.613 GB (12.68 GiB); the token is **16.506 GB**, not 14.00 GB. The 0.53125
   B/param constant now applies only to the seven quantized linears, and w4a8 is 0.515625 B/param.
2. **VRAM reconciled.** 15.01 GiB of weights + 0.74 GiB of KV/arena/runtime = the measured 15.75 GiB
   (C41); cross-validated by the 2.04 GiB drop measured when `attn.qg/o` were quantized (predicted
   2.07). The previous ~3 GiB of unexplained footprint was the bf16 tensors.
3. **Decode ceiling corrected from "43-46 tok/s" to 36.6 tok/s** (604 GB/s measured, corrected
   bytes), with 38.8 as an unreachable spec bound. C8's 641-649 GB/s is removed from every ceiling
   derivation and re-labelled as an unverified, cache-flattered [TUNE] figure ([BENCH §5]'s own
   caveat). **w4a16 is at 89.6% of its roofline, not 75.6%** -- kernel work on this card is capped
   at +12%.
4. **§2.5 no longer rescales percentages onto the wrong step time.** Both clocks are stated, the
   per-block table uses [PERF]'s unmodified ms, and the constant +5.4 ms offset between the profiled
   and steady-state steps is promoted to Q2. Gap split is now **GDN 46% / MLP 38% / attention 13%**,
   not 80/7/15; "the MLP is done at 97% of roofline" is withdrawn (it is at 81%).
5. **[TUNE] is no longer used as an in-model cost model anywhere** (§2.5's per-layout remainder
   table, §2.6's 523 us/token). The mxfp4 deficit is restated as two candidate causes (scalar fp8
   quant kernel; ~1.5x cache flattery on mxfp4 `down`/`qkv`), separated by Q4/Q5, and the per-quant-
   call cost is re-derived from the MLP block's byte roofline instead.

   raises the ceiling (36.6 -> 43.0 tok/s). Gated by new accuracy question Q14.
7. **Launch census re-derived from `linear.cpp` / `gdn_layer.cpp`**: 433 GEMMs (257 quantized + 176
   bf16), **257** quant/cast launches (not 224), ~510 other, ~1200 total. P2's
   "quantize-once-per-producer-row" idea is **withdrawn**: no duplicate quantization exists, because
   every linear that shares a normed row with another is bf16.
