# W8A16: an 8-bit weight-only layout (design and outcome)

**Status: implemented and validated 2026-10-16.** The converter, the kernel, the loader and the LFM2 path are merged to
`main`. The sections below are the original design, kept as written; the "Outcome" section that follows records what was
measured and where the build differs from the text. Where a design sentence says UNVERIFIED or gives an estimate, the
measurement in "Outcome" replaces it.

### Deviations from the design

1. **TP=2 is refused in v1.** Section 6.2 allowed w8a16 at TP=2 ("not a v1 deliverable", gate optional). The build refuses
   `--layout w8a16` with `--tp 2`; the layout is TP=1 only. The container byte layout still slices by whole row tiles and
   whole K blocks, so TP support remains a loader and shard-table job, not a format change.
2. **Kernel LDS cap in the fallback SK rule.** Section 4.5 chose SK as the largest of {8, 4, 2, 1} with
   `K % (SK*G) == 0` and `WV*SK <= 32`. The built `W8a16FallbackTuning` (src/model/linear.cpp) also requires the block's
   LDS reduction `WV*NPW*SK` KiB <= 64, the kernel's own launch cap (an SK of 8 at NPW 4 and WV 4 would be 128 KiB).
   `W8a16RowFits` mirrors the same rule for tuning-table rows.
3. **Header names.** The kernel C ABI lives in `src/kernels/include/r4dx/kernels/w8a16_gemm.h` (the lane contract name).
   `w8a16_kernels.h`, the name section 4.1 uses, is a forwarding header that includes it. The on-disk constants (group sizes,
   scale bias, tensor suffixes, the metadata strings) are in a new dependency-free header,
   `src/convert/include/r4dx_convert/w8a16_format.hpp`, which the design did not list; the converter, the loader and the
   kernel tests all include it.
4. **LDLQ deferred.** Section 5.3's `SymInt8Policy` and the `--ldlq` path for w8a16 were not built. The gate passed with
   RTN, so the fallback was not needed.

- Tuning rows: run `tests\kernels\tool_w8a16_gemm_bench.exe --inc-out` on the LFM2 shapes and fill

The first target is LFM2.5-24B-A2B on **one** GPU. The layout is generic, so the Qwen and Gemma loaders get it for free
through the shared loader (section 6).

`w8a16` means int8 weights with f16 group scales, bf16 activations in memory, f32 accumulation and a bf16 output. It is a
fourth `--layout` next to `bf16 | w4a16 | trellis`. Section 3.4 explains why the WMMA operand is f16 and not bf16. The
bf16 to f16 conversion is exact, so the numerics are those of a bf16 GEMM over dequantized weights.

The file references are to `main` at 7cd002f.

Variants: a bf16 head gives 25.13 GB. Per-channel scales give 24.50 GB. g64 gives 25.25 GB. The "non-expert linears in
bf16" fallback (section 5.2) gives about 25.9 GB. For comparison, the bf16 container is 48.5 GB (TP=2 only) and the
shipped trellis hybrid is 18.89 GB.

**VRAM (estimate, UNVERIFIED).** The trellis hybrid measured a 20.29 GiB peak with a 17.59 GiB container at 8k context,
which leaves about 2.7 GiB for KV, arena and logits. KV is bf16 on 10 layers: 20,480 B per token, so 2.5 GiB at 131072.
The w8a16 estimate is therefore about **25.9 GiB at 8k and about 28.2 GiB at 128k**. That fits device 1. Device 0 (the
desktop card, about 2 GB taken) fits up to roughly 64k.

**Quality (expected about bf16).** Measured on real LFM2 tensors with a CPU probe that read 9 single tensors (layers 0,
2, 20 and 39: conv in/out, dense down, attn out, expert w1/w2). The relative Frobenius error of symmetric RTN int8 was:

- g128: 0.0065-0.0070 on every tensor sampled;
- per output channel: 0.0083-0.0102.

For comparison, int4 g64 asymmetric is about 0.08-0.10 (tests/convert/test_quantize_roundtrip.cpp bound 0.13). The
all-K5 trellis (5.02 bpw) cost +0.0156 ALL KL over bf16 (docs/lfm2.md). The squared error of int8 g128 is more than an
order of magnitude below a 5-bit grid, so the **expected ALL increment is about 0.001-0.003 (UNVERIFIED)**. The gate is
the existing quantized rule, increment <= 0.01 per group (section 7.4). The design target is ALL <= 0.003 and top-1
within 0.3 points of r4dx bf16.

**Speed: no gain without L4.** Bytes per decoded token (active experts 4 x 38 x 9.44 M, plus every non-expert linear,
plus the head) are about **2.49 GB at w8a16**. The shipped hybrid moves about 3.1 GB, because its non-expert linears,
head and layers 2-5 experts are bf16. So w8a16 moves fewer bytes per token than the hybrid. Decode today is
host-sync and launch bound, though: the staged MoE path in `Lfm2MoeFfn::Forward` (src/model/lfm2_layers.cpp) does one
`hipStreamSynchronize` per MoE layer (38 per token) and about 410 GEMM launches per token. That is why trellis TP=1 and
bf16 TP=2 both sit at about 61 tok/s. **Expect about 60 tok/s from w8a16 as is.** The ceiling at the measured effective
rate of about 300 GB/s is about 120 tok/s, and reaching it needs plan phase L4 (device-indexed grouped MoE decode, section
4.6). The in-register activation conversion of section 3.4 at least avoids adding about 300 cast launches per token,
which a w4a16-style pre-cast would cost.

## 2. Quantization scheme

| choice | decision | reason |
|---|---|---|
| grid | symmetric int8, codes -127..127 (-128 unused), no zero point | 8 bits leave nothing for an asymmetric zero to win; no zero means no per-group subtract in the kernel |
| granularity | **per group of G = 128 along K**, per output row; G = 64 is also instantiated | measured above: g128 has 25-35 % less error than per-channel for +1.6 % bytes |
| scale | **f16**, one per (row, group), stored **biased by 2^12** (`scale_log2_bias = 12`) | see below |
| rounding | **RTN** (round half away from zero, float32, `RoundHalfAwayFromZero` in quant_int4.hpp) | near-lossless at 8 bits; LDLQ is a prepared fallback (5.3) |
| zero groups | a group whose biased scale flushes to f16 zero is written as scale 0, codes 0, and counted in the log | real LFM2 rows have amax about 1e-23 (2.3 % of the groups of `layers.2.experts.0.w1`) |

**Why f16 with a bias.** The real scale distribution is amax/127 = 1.3e-4 .. 5.6e-3 (median about 3e-4), with
outliers at 4.7e-5. That sits at the bottom of f16's normal range (6.1e-5), and with the dead rows near 1e-25. Unbiased
f16 would put some scales into subnormals, where precision falls to a few bits. It would also make
`EncodeScaleF16Checked`'s "flushed to 0" check abort the conversion on the dead rows. Multiplying by 2^12 maps the live
range to about 0.2 .. 23 and keeps every amax up to 2032 representable. The kernel undoes the bias with one exact
power-of-two multiply. The bias is a **format constant**: the converter writes it into the metadata, and the loader
refuses any other value (6.3). fp32 scales would also work, at +0.37 GB with no bias. bf16 scales are rejected, because a
2^-9 gain error per group is a third of the rounding noise.

**The quantizer (exact, mirrored bit for bit by `tools/convert_ref/w8_ref.py`):**

```
amax  = max |w| over the group (float32)
s     = amax / 127
s16   = f16(s * 2^12)                     # RNE; inf -> error naming the tensor
if s16 == 0: scale 0, every code 0, ++zero_groups
s_use = float(s16) * 2^-12                # quantize against the STORED scale, not s
q     = clamp(RoundHalfAwayFromZero(w / s_use), -127, 127)
byte  = uint8(q + 128)                    # offset binary, 1..255
```

**Which rounding, and how to measure it.**

- **Step 0, CPU, minutes.** `tools/reference/w8_proxy.py` (lane W4) computes, for a sample of linears, the relative
  Frobenius error and the Hessian-weighted proxy tr(dW H dW^T) / tr(W H W^T). The sample is every non-expert linear plus
  all experts of layers 2, 5, 20 and 39, with H from `D:\r4dx-scratch\lfm2\hessian-v1`. That directory's `hessian.json`
  maps container base names to files, for example `text.layers.2.moe.experts.0.gate_up` to `L02.E00.mlp_in.hess`. The
  schemes are per-channel, g128 and g64 RTN, and g128 LDLQ. Each process stays under 4 GB.
- **Step 1, the decision.** The real container at RTN g128, then the KL gate (7.4).
- **LDLQ.** Only if the gate misses, and only on the classes the proxy flags. The obvious candidates are the experts of
  layers 2-5, by analogy with the trellis layer sweep. LDLQ uses the same Hessians, already captured with the routed
  per-expert keys.

## 3. Container tensor layout

### 3.1 Tensors and metadata

For a linear `<base>` with W [N, K] at group G:

| tensor | dtype on disk | planned shape | bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `<base>.w8a16.wq` | u8 | `{N*K}` | N*K |
| `<base>.w8a16.ws` | u8 (f16 pairs) | `{N*K/G, 2}` | N*K/G*2 |

The shape convention follows w4a16's `.w4a16.wq` / `.w4a16.wsz` in `PlanLinearLayouts`. Header block, written by
`BuildQuantMetadata` in src/convert/main.cpp whenever any linear is w8a16:

```json
"quant": { "w8a16": { "version": 1, "group": 128, "scale": "f16", "scale_log2_bias": 12,
                      "code": "int8_sym127_offset_binary_x80",
                      "fragment_permutation": "wmma16x16x16_f16_b_lane16_k32block" } }
```

`groups` (a per-linear map like `quant.w4a16.groups`) is **not** in v1. One group per container is enough, since
`--w8a16-group` is run-wide (5.1). A loader without w4a16's legacy problem simply checks every scale tensor's byte size
against N*K/G*2, so a wrong group can never be read at the wrong stride.

### 3.2 Byte order (the kernel's fragment order)

The WMMA here is **wave32** (`WAVE32 32` and `__builtin_amdgcn_wmma_f32_16x16x16_f16_w32_gfx12` in
third_party/libr4d/r4d_gemm_w4a16_nt_m64.hip), not wave64. The f16 B-fragment map is the one in w4a16's header
comment (from r4d_gdn_wmma.h): lane l, element e = 0..7 of k-step s holds `W[n0 + (l&15)][16s + 8*(e>>2) + 4*(l>>4) +
(e&3)]`. Elements 0..3 and 4..7 are each **four contiguous K**, so a lane's eight bytes per k-step are two dwords:
d0 = e0..e3 and d1 = e4..e7.

Packed block = 16 rows x **32 K** = 512 bytes = one `global_load_b128` per lane (2 k-steps). Dword index and contents:

```
dword  ((t*(K/32) + kb)*32 + l)*4 + j          t = row tile (N/16), kb = 32-K block, l = lane 0..31, j = 0..3
row    = 16t + (l & 15)
byte i (0..3) of dword j holds code(row, k) with
k      = 32kb + 16*(j>>1) + 8*(j&1) + 4*(l>>4) + i
```

Each row tile is then one contiguous run of 16*K bytes, which gives TP row slicing for free (6.2).

Scales: u16 index `((t*(K/G) + g)*16 + r)`, the f16 bits of `s * 2^12` for row 16t + r and group g. Lane l reads row
`l & 15` (lanes 16..31 duplicate), which is the same addressing as w4a16's `ps[j]`.

### 3.3 Divisibility (checked by the converter at plan time, the loader, and the kernel)

- N % 16 == 0.
- K % G == 0 (which implies K % 32 for G in {64, 128}).
- A launch also needs K % (SK * G) == 0, so every K split starts on a group.

LFM2 at TP=1: the K values are **2048** (attn q/k/v/o, conv in_proj/out_proj, both gate_ups, lm_head), **1536** (expert
down) and **11776** (dense down). 6144 is conv in_proj's **N**, not a K. At G=128 these are 16, 12 and 92 groups, all
divisible by SK = 4. At TP=2 the row-parallel K values are 768 (6 groups), 5888 (46) and
1024 (8), legal at SK in {1, 2} (768, 5888) and up to 8 (1024). Every N, rank-local included (256 for attn k/v at TP=2,
64000 for the head), is a multiple of 16.

### 3.4 How the weight is widened (the decision)

| option | per 8-element B fragment | numerics | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **f16 WMMA, magic exponent**: `v_perm_b32(0x64646464, d, sel)` makes `0x64XX` = 1024 + byte (exact for every byte 0..255), then one `v_pk_add_f16` with (-1152, -1152) gives q exactly | 4 perm + 4 pk_add = **8 VALU**, no masks (w4a16 needs about 10) | exact weight; A must be f16 | **chosen** |
| bf16 WMMA, int8 to bf16 | the bf16 magic trick covers only 7 bits; there is no packed bf16 add on gfx1201 (w4a16's header), so through f32: about 20 VALU | exact | rejected: 2.5x the unpack |
| `v_wmma_i32_16x16x16_iu8` / `v_dot4_i32_i8` | cheapest, 2x the WMMA rate | needs **quantized activations** (W8A8) | rejected for W8A16; a possible later prefill-only W8A8 experiment with its own KL gate (cf. docs/int8-prefill.md) |

The selectors are w4a16's own: `perm(K64, d0, 0x05010400)` gives (e0, e1) and `0x05030402` gives (e2, e3); the same on
d1 gives e4..e7.

**Activations: bf16 in memory, f16 in the WMMA.** bf16 to f16 is **exact** for |x| in [2^-14, 65504], because bf16's
8-bit significand fits in f16's 11. The kernel converts in registers: `lo = d << 16`, `hi = d & 0xffff0000`, then
`v_cvt_pk_rtz_f16_f32(lo, hi)`. That is 3 VALU per pair. RTZ changes nothing for an in-range value. On overflow it
yields the largest finite (+-65504) instead of inf, which is the saturation the design wants; the kernel test proves it
on hardware (7.2). Below 2^-14, f16 subnormals lose bits whose absolute size is under 6e-8. Doing this in-kernel instead
of w4a16's separate `r4dx_model_cast_bf16_to_f16` launch (src/model/linear.cpp, `ApplyLinear`, case kW4a16) saves one
launch and one arena buffer per GEMM. That matters at decode: 4 experts x 2 GEMMs x 38 layers.

**Scale application** is w4a16's: a per-group f32 accumulator `g_acc` starts at the group's first WMMA. At the group's
end, `acc = fmaf(g_acc, sc, acc)` with `sc = float(s16)` read once per group. The final `acc * 2^-12` (exact) happens
before the bf16 store. Products f16 x int8 (11 x 8 bits) are exact in the f32 WMMA accumulator. Do not fold the scale
into the fragment, for the reason recorded in w4a16's "REFUTED" comment.

## 4. Kernel plan

### 4.1 Where it lives: `src/kernels`, not libr4d

New TU `src/kernels/src/w8a16_gemm.hip` and header `src/kernels/include/r4dx/kernels/w8a16_kernels.h`. Build block in
src/kernels/CMakeLists.txt in the lfm2_kernels / rep_penalty pattern: its own `add_custom_command` with
`${R4DX_KERNELS_FLAGS}` (the same `-O3 -ffp-contract=off` as libr4d's `R4D_BASE_FLAGS`), attached by
`target_sources(r4dx_kernels PRIVATE ...)`, so the `add_library` line is not touched. It includes libr4d's
`r4d_gdn_wmma.h` (`rowGo`, `v8f`) through the `-I third_party/libr4d` already there. Consequences:

- **No libr4d change**: no `R4D_UNITS` entry (third_party/CMakeLists.txt), no `r4d.h` declaration, no `r4d_registry.hip`
  row, no libr4d version bump. Qwen and Gemma's libr4d objects are byte-identical.
- An optional `-S` resource report, like the trellis ISA check (third_party/CMakeLists.txt, `-DWQ=ON`, reported and not
  gated): VGPR count and no scratch, per instantiation.

### 4.2 Entry points (C ABI, int64_t pointers like the r4dx_* kernels)

```c
// C[M,N] bf16 = A[M,K] bf16 @ dequant(W)[N,K]^T; 1 <= M <= 64; group in {64,128}; scale bias 2^12.
void r4dx_gemm_w8a16_nt_m64(int64_t a_bf16, int64_t wq, int64_t ws, int64_t c, int M, int K, int N, int group,
                            int WV, int SK, int MB, int NPW, int NT, int64_t stream);
int  r4dx_gemm_w8a16_has_group(int group);        // 64, 128
int  r4dx_gemm_w8a16_scale_log2_bias(void);       // 12 (the converter and loader assert it)
int  r4dx_gemm_w8a16_max_m(void);                 // 64
```

Throws `std::runtime_error` on an illegal launch, using the w4a16 launcher's rules: M in 1..64, N % 16,
K % (SK * G), WV*SK*32 <= 1024, MB 1..4, NPW in {1, 4}, LDS WV*NPW*SK KiB <= 64.

### 4.3 Structure: a copy of `r4d_gemm_w4a16_nt_m64_kernel` with three changes

Template `<int G, int MT, int NPW, bool NT>`. The decomposition is w4a16's: WV column-tile groups x SK K-splits per
block, MT (= MB) row tiles per block, NPW column tiles per wave sharing one A fragment, and the fixed-order LDS split-K
reduction ("Split-K reduction and store"). Row tiles past M are clamped, never masked. The split-K is **inside one
block** (waves), like w4a16. There are **no tickets** and no cross-block partials (those are trellis's, docs/trellis-kernel.md
4.5), so nothing needs zeroing and two launches of the same linear may overlap. The changes:

1. **Weight load**: one b128 per lane per **32-K block** (2 k-steps), so a group of 128 is 4 blocks and 8 k-steps.
   `pw[j] = Wq + (t*(K/32) + kbase/32)*32 + lane` (uint4 units).
2. **Unpack**: `dequant8(d0, d1)` = the 4 perm + 4 `pk_add(-1152)` of 3.4, with no loaded zero-point dword.
3. **A operand**: `fragA` reads bf16 (the same two u64 loads) and converts in registers to f16 (3.4).
   `pa[i] += (G/16)*4` per group, as in w4a16.

**Bit properties the callers rely on.** These are the same as w4a16, and the tests pin them:

- A row's result depends on SK alone, not on WV/MB/NPW/NT or on the other rows. That is what makes a verify window's
  rows equal the M=1 row (`kRowTile` in linear.cpp), and LFM2's `kPerSlot` equal to `kSorted` for experts with <= 16
  rows.
- Runs are deterministic.

### 4.4 Decode (M = 1..16) and prefill (M up to 256)

- **Decode**: the same kernel at M <= 16, one row tile. `ResolveTuning` already routes M=2..16 to the M=1 band
  (linear.cpp). w8a16 takes the same rule, NT = 0 for M > 1, as w4a16 does.
- **Prefill**: there is **no M=256 w8a16 kernel in v1**. `ApplyLinear` already chunks M into 64-row slices
  (`kMaxChunkM`). That is the w4a16 structure; libr4d has no w4a16 M=256 kernel, and M=256 exists only for trellis
  (`r4d_gemm_trellis_nt_m256`). This is justified for LFM2: the sorted MoE path calls the experts with c = that expert's
  rows (about 16 per 256-row chunk on average). Only the about 0.75 B non-expert parameters ever see M=256. MoE prefill is
  weight-bandwidth bound per chunk (all 64 experts read per 256-row chunk is about 24 GB). Its lever is plan L4-4 (grouped
  prefill GEMM, larger MoE chunks), not a wide kernel.
- **Grouped MoE decode (phase 2, with L4-2).** A device-indexed variant `r4dx_gemm_w8a16_moe_decode` takes the router's
  `ids` on device and two per-layer pointer tables (wq, ws) per expert, like `Lfm2MoeWeights::gate_up_bf16_ptrs`
  (lfm2_container.cpp's `ptr_table`). The slot index goes in grid.z. It removes the 38 syncs per token. It shares
  everything with 4.3 except the pointer lookup. This is designed with L4-2's bf16 kernel, not before it.

### 4.5 Tuning tables

- New `src/model/gemm_tuning_table_w8a16.inc` (namespace `w8a16`, included by linear.cpp like the trellis tables). It
  starts as a one-row placeholder with N == 0 (a C++ array cannot be empty; the `_trellis_i8_tp2.inc` precedent). Rows
  use `GemmTuningRow::group` as the key, as w4a16 does.
- `FallbackTuning` gets a **w8a16 branch**, because the generic K % 256 rule is wrong for it:
  - SK = the largest of {8, 4, 2, 1} with K % (SK*G) == 0 and WV*SK <= 32;
  - WV = 1 when N/16 < 512 (the expert linears: N 3072 / 2048 would otherwise launch only 32-48 blocks on a 64-CU part),
    else 4;
  - M <= 16: MB 1, NPW 1, NT 1;
  - M > 16: MB = min(4, ceil(M/16)), NPW 4, NT 0.

  These are starting points (UNVERIFIED), replaced by measured rows.
- `PickTuning`'s cache key: `Layout::kW8a16 = 5` (bits 48..51) and the group in the variant bits 53+ (128 < 512).
- `tests/kernels/tool_w8a16_gemm_bench.exe --inc-out` (lane W2) writes the rows: an M=1 row per (N, K, G) and M=32/64
  rows, at TP=1 and the TP=2 rank shapes.

## 5. Converter

### 5.1 Flags

- `--layouts w8a16` / `--lm-head w8a16`: `ParseLayoutList` (main.cpp) accepts the token `w8a16`, alone or with others.
  `--layouts` keeps its "bf16 on by default" rule, so **an LFM2 w8a16 run must pass `--no-bf16`**, or it writes 48 + 25
  GB. A w8a16 + bf16 pair on one linear is legal: no old binary can request `w8a16`, because `LayoutFromName` throws, so
  w4a16's companion-guard reasoning does not apply.
- `--w8a16-group {64,128}` (default 128), run-wide. A `--w8a16-group-rule` mirroring `W4a16GroupRules` is deferred until a
  measurement asks for per-class groups.
- `--quant search` / `--imatrix` do not apply to w8a16 in v1. They are an argument error when w8a16 is the only quantized
  layout, so a log never claims an imatrix-weighted container that is not.
- `--ldlq <regex> --hessian-dir <dir>` applies to w8a16 (5.3). Precedence is unchanged: `--keep-bf16` wins.
- `--trellis-from`: a trellis linear is exclusive (`CheckTrellisLayout` refuses w8a16 next to it). Every other linear
  follows `--layouts` as today.

The layout slices like w4a16, because row tiles are contiguous and K blocks are fixed-size:

### 6.3 Refusals (each a test row)

1. `--layout w8a16` on a container **without** `quant.w8a16` is refused before any upload. Otherwise every linear would
   fall back and one card would try to load 48 GB of bf16.
2. `quant.w8a16` present, with an unknown version or code string, an uninstantiated group, or a bias != the kernel's.
3. A `.w8a16.ws` whose size is not N*K/G*2 (or `.wq` not N*K), naming the tensor.
4. A TP rank range that is not whole tiles / groups (`PlanRows` / `PlanCols` already throw, naming the part).
5. An old binary (any build before this layout) given `--layout w8a16`: `LayoutFromName` throws
   "unrecognized layout". Given `--layout bf16` on a `--no-bf16` w8a16 container: "no tensor found ... in any known
   on-disk form". Both are by construction, with no new code.
6. The converter refuses: w8a16 next to trellis, `--quant search` / `--imatrix` with w8a16 as the only quantized layout,
   any w8a16 flag with `--dflash-gguf`, and a shape that fails 3.3, naming the linear.

## 9. Risks and open questions

1. **f16 activation range (UNVERIFIED for LFM2).** An activation above 65504 saturates instead of overflowing (3.4),
   but it is still wrong. Nothing in docs/lfm2-semantics.md records activation amax. Probe it once: the max |x| at every
   linear input on the KL corpus, from the bf16 TP=2 run. If anything reaches about 1e4, the fallback is a bf16-WMMA
   variant for that class only (slower unpack, 3.4).
2. **Speed is gated on L4** (section 1). As built, w8a16 is a quality and footprint win (about 25 GB on one card at about
   bf16 quality, against the 18.9 GB hybrid's +0.0055) at about the same 60 tok/s. The phase-2 device-indexed expert
   kernel is where its smaller byte count turns into tokens per second.
3. **Small-N decode occupancy.** The expert linears have only 128-192 row tiles. The fallback's WV=1 / SK up to 8 is a
   guess; the bench decides.
4. **Scale format.** f16 with a fixed 2^12 bias is chosen over fp32 (+0.37 GB). If a future model has weight groups with
   amax > 2032, the converter fails loudly, and the fix is a new `scale_log2_bias` value (a format version bump), not a
   silent clamp.
5. **The head in w8a16** is not separately validated by the trellis work, which kept a bf16 head. If the gate attributes
   KL to it, use `--lm-head bf16` (+0.26 GB).
6. **Mixed trellis + w8a16 container** (trellis K5 experts with w8a16 non-expert and layers 2-5 experts) could pass the
   gate at about 16-17 GB. It needs a loader fallback chain trellis -> w8a16 -> bf16, which `LoadQuantLinearWithFallback`
   does not have (requested -> bf16 -> bare). This is a deliberate non-goal of v1, to decide after 7.4.
7. **The embedding** stays bf16 (0.52 GB). Gathering rows from the w8a16 head bytes would save it but needs a dequant
   gather; deferred.
8. **No W8A8.** The 2x-rate integer WMMA needs activation quantization and is a separate prefill experiment with its own
   gate.
9. **VRAM on device 0** at long context is tight (section 1). Measure the peak at 128k before advertising one-card
   128k on device 0.
10. **Per-channel vs g128** is decided by measurement (step 0 plus the gate), not by the probe in section 1 alone. That
    probe covered 9 tensors.
